How to survive a three-night college fest

Sleep, snacks and squad plans: a practical guide to getting the most from Utsav, 8th Mile and every big fest in between.

FestHey EditorialUpdated Nov 4, 2026 · 6 min read

On this page

Share this

Big college fests run for days, across stages, labs and grounds. A little planning turns them from a blur into the best weekend of the semester.

Key takeaways

  • Pick two headline nights and pace yourself
  • Create team registrations early and share one link
  • Carry ID; gates check it more than once
  • Book your ride home before the encore

Plan your nights before day one

Three-night fests are marathons. Pick the two headline nights you care most about and treat the third as optional. Most regret comes from burning out on night one.

Check the schedule on the fest page and save the events you want, so reminders arrive on your registered email.

Use the schedule, not the poster

Posters show headliners; the schedule shows clashes. Two events you love can overlap by twenty minutes, and the fest page flags it before you register.

Sort the squad and the money

Team events need every member registered before checkout. Create the team early, share the invite link, and let each person pay their own share.

Where there is a fee, the amount and the convenience fee are shown before you pay, so there are no surprises.

One captain, one link

The captain names the team once and shares a single invite link. Teammates join in a tap and see exactly what they owe.

The best fest weekend is the one you planned on Thursday.An organiser at Utsav

Pack like a regular

Many campuses check ID at every gate, not just the first one. Travel light and keep the essentials in one pocket.

  • College ID and your QR pass
  • A charged power bank
  • Refillable water bottle
  • Some cash for food stalls

After the last act

Plan the ride home before the pro-night ends. Campus gates get crowded, and cabs surge right after the final encore.

Leave a review of the events you joined. It helps organisers improve and helps next year’s students pick well.
